# Identifying AI Risks for Non-Human Life in Urban Spaces

AI systems deployed in streets and parks can drastically alter how non-human life survives in cities, yet standard AI safety frameworks remain highly anthropocentric. This project provides a clear, systematic map of AI-related harms across the full range of non-human life in urban environments by bridging AI deployment logic with ecological risk assessment.

## My Contributions
* Helped design a three-step pipeline (using GPT models and Python) to model spatial, sensory, and kinematic overlaps between 257 urban AI systems and 262 non-human organisms.
* Helped design counterfactual auditing logic (evaluating direct causes and alternate explanations) to extract causal failure patterns from high-risk scenarios.
* Co-authored and organized the final analysis, highlighting the key findings of the research.
* Wrote several sections of the project report/paper.

## Methodology & Key Findings
* **The Pipeline:** Compiled biological data across 10 strict ecological dimensions, generated 23,945 potential risk interactions via generative LLM elicitation, and used a deterministic Python script to filter the top 10% (90th percentile) for counterfactual auditing.
* **Blind Spots:** Proved that AI systems disproportionately cause harm to organisms with small physical scales (55.6%) and erratic movement (44.4%).
* **Regulatory Misalignment:** Identified a "Regulatory Blind Spot", revealing that AI systems classified as 'Limited or Low Risk' under the EU AI Act actually carry higher average ecological severity scores (2.82) than 'High Risk' systems (2.57).
